Routine clinical measurements may help prioritise further examinations in occupational health programmes, but repeated attendance and selective testing complicate model evaluation. We assessed how well recorded findings can be predicted across occupational screening groups and over time. We analysed 36,885 visits from linked routine clinic, laboratory, and imaging records of an Iranian occupational health programme (2019-2026). Twelve recorded outcomes were modelled using 50 predictors and evaluated under three held-out schemes: reconstructed identities (birth date and sex), employer- or role-defined screening groups, and later calendar years. Mean within-group AUROCs were 0.774 (95% conditional CI, 0.757-0.790) for prostate echotexture and 0.733 (0.715-0.750) for recorded low bone mass. Under temporal validation, AUROCs decreased to 0.711 and 0.628, with calibration slopes of 0.516 and 0.422, indicating overly extreme risk estimates. Pooled metrics could mask poor within-group ranking. Refitting on first recorded visits yielded mean group-level AUROCs of 0.748 and 0.795, although few groups were eligible. Routine measurements carried target-dependent predictive information, but performance varied across screening groups and deteriorated over time. Any referral policy would require adjudicated outcome labels, verification of predictor availability, and independent validation of fixed thresholds. Because true person-level linkage and prospective predictor availability could not be established, these findings are exploratory.
